State Income Tax Guide

State income tax is one of the biggest reasons take-home pay can differ across the United States. Two people with the same gross salary may keep very different amounts after tax depending on where they live.

This guide explains how state income tax works, why after-tax salary varies by location, and how to compare states when evaluating job offers, relocation options, or long-term income planning.

Why State Income Tax Matters

Federal income tax applies across the country, but state tax rules are different in every state. Some states do not charge personal income tax at all, while others use flat or progressive systems.

Because of these differences, the same salary can produce meaningfully different net income depending on location. This matters when comparing compensation, planning a move, or deciding whether a higher gross salary is actually worth it.

States With No State Income Tax

Some U.S. states are known for having no personal state income tax. These states can sometimes produce higher take-home pay, although total cost of living and other taxes should still be considered.

  • Texas
  • Florida
  • Washington
  • Nevada
  • Tennessee
  • South Dakota
  • Wyoming
  • Alaska

States With Higher Income Tax Burden

Some states apply higher effective tax rates, especially at higher income levels. These states may result in lower take-home pay even when gross salary appears attractive.

  • California
  • New York
  • Oregon
  • Minnesota
  • New Jersey

A higher-tax state is not always a worse choice overall, but after-tax income should always be part of the comparison.

How to Compare States for Salary Planning

When comparing states, do not look only at gross salary. A realistic comparison should consider:

  • Federal income tax
  • State income tax
  • Social Security and Medicare deductions
  • Housing and cost of living differences
  • Whether the state uses flat or progressive income tax rules

This is why after-tax salary calculators are useful. They make it easier to compare net income between states before making a decision.

Example State Comparison

Imagine a salary of $80,000:

  • In Texas, there is no state income tax, so take-home pay may be higher.
  • In California, state income tax applies, which reduces net annual income.
